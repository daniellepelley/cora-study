# Maths: Pearson Edexcel A level Mathematics (9MA0)

> Official spec, past papers and mark schemes: search "Pearson Edexcel A level Mathematics 9MA0" on qualifications.pearson.com. Check details against the current official specification.

## How it is examined (all at the end of Year 13)

| Paper | Content | Time | Marks | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: Pure Mathematics 1 | Any pure content | 2 h | 100 | 33⅓% |
| Paper 2: Pure Mathematics 2 | Any pure content | 2 h | 100 | 33⅓% |
| Paper 3: Statistics and Mechanics | Section A Statistics (50), Section B Mechanics (50) | 2 h | 100 | 33⅓% |

- Calculators are allowed in all papers. Cora should know her calculator well, including its statistical distribution functions.
- The **"Mathematical Formulae and Statistical Tables"** booklet is provided. She still needs to know the formulae that are *not* in it.
- **Large Data Set (LDS):** Edexcel's weather data. Paper 3 Statistics expects familiarity with it. Ask her teacher which version applies to her exam series.
- Mark-scheme conventions: **M** (method), **A** (accuracy, depends on M), **B** (independent), **dM** (method mark that depends on an earlier M). "Show that" means every step must be shown. "Hence" means the previous result must be used.

## Content

### Pure
1. Proof (deduction, exhaustion, counter-example, contradiction)
2. Algebra and functions (indices, surds, quadratics, inequalities, polynomials and the factor theorem, graphs and transformations, modulus, composite and inverse functions, partial fractions)
3. Coordinate geometry (straight lines, circles, parametric equations)
4. Sequences and series (binomial expansion, including fractional/negative n; arithmetic and geometric series; sigma notation; recurrence relations)
5. Trigonometry (radians, arc length and sector area, small angles, identities, reciprocal and inverse trig functions, compound and double angles, R cos(θ ± α))
6. Exponentials and logarithms (laws of logs, eˣ and ln x, modelling, using log graphs to estimate parameters)
7. Differentiation (first principles, chain, product and quotient rules, implicit and parametric, connected rates of change, stationary points, concavity and points of inflection)
8. Integration (standard integrals, substitution, by parts, partial fractions, areas, trapezium rule, differential equations)
9. Numerical methods (locating roots, iteration, Newton-Raphson)
10. Vectors (2D and 3D)

### Statistics
1. Statistical sampling
2. Data presentation and interpretation (including the LDS, correlation and regression)
3. Probability (Venn and tree diagrams, conditional probability)
4. Statistical distributions (binomial, normal, normal approximation)
5. Statistical hypothesis testing (binomial, correlation coefficient, normal mean)

### Mechanics
1. Quantities and units
2. Kinematics (suvat, variable acceleration using calculus, projectiles)
3. Forces and Newton's laws (including friction, connected particles, inclined planes)
4. Moments

## Links with her other subjects
- **Physics:** mechanics, vectors, calculus, exponentials and logs (radioactive decay, capacitor discharge), log-linear graphs. These overlap heavily, so practise them together.
- **Biology:** statistics (correlation, hypothesis testing), logs (population growth, pH-style scales).
