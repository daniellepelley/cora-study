# Biology: OCR A level Biology A (H420)

> ⚠️ OCR has two A level Biology specifications: **Biology A (H420)**, which most schools use, and **Biology B: Advancing Biology (H422)**. This file assumes **H420**. Confirm with Cora's teacher and rewrite this file if she is on H422.
>
> Official spec, past papers and mark schemes: ocr.org.uk, "A Level Biology A H420". Check details against the current official specification.

## How it is examined (all at the end of Year 13)

| Paper | Modules assessed | Time | Marks | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 Biological processes | 1, 2, 3, 5 | 2 h 15 min | 100 | 37% |
| 02 Biological diversity | 1, 2, 4, 6 | 2 h 15 min | 100 | 37% |
| 03 Unified biology | 1–6 | 1 h 30 min | 70 | 26% |

- Papers 01 and 02 start with multiple-choice questions, then structured and extended-response questions.
- Extended responses marked with an asterisk (\*) are **levels-of-response** questions. Marks depend on the quality and logical structure of the whole answer, not just the facts.
- At least **10% of marks test maths skills**: statistics such as chi-squared, Student's t-test, Spearman's rank and standard deviation, plus rates, percentage change and logs.
- **Practical Endorsement** (reported separately as Pass/Not classified). Assessed in school through the **PAGs** (Practical Activity Groups 1–12). Practical skills are also examined in the written papers (Module 1).

## Content

1. **Development of practical skills in biology.** Planning, implementing, analysing, evaluating. Tested across all papers.
2. **Foundations in biology.** Cell structure (microscopy), biological molecules, nucleotides and nucleic acids, enzymes, biological membranes, cell division, cell diversity and cellular organisation.
3. **Exchange and transport.** Exchange surfaces (gas exchange), transport in animals (heart, blood vessels), transport in plants.
4. **Biodiversity, evolution and disease.** Communicable diseases and the immune system, biodiversity, classification and evolution.
5. **Communication, homeostasis and energy.** Communication and homeostasis, excretion (liver, kidney), neuronal communication, hormonal communication, plant and animal responses, photosynthesis, respiration.
6. **Genetics, evolution and ecosystems.** Cellular control, patterns of inheritance (including chi-squared), manipulating genomes, cloning and biotechnology, ecosystems, populations and sustainability.

Modules 1–4 are usually taught in Year 12 and modules 5–6 in Year 13. Check the school's order.

## Notes
- Biological molecules, enzymes, respiration and photosynthesis rely on some chemistry. If Cora isn't taking A-level Chemistry, the tutor should fill those gaps as they come up (bonding, pH, energy, redox).
- OCR mark schemes reward **precise technical vocabulary**. Vague answers such as "it gets used up" or "the enzyme dies" lose marks.
